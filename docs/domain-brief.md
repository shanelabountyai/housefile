# Housefile — Domain Brief (CRM data-quality review)

The source for vocabulary, the synthetic population, the defect catalog, normalization, blocking, the scoring model, survivorship, undo semantics and the golden pairs. Amounts are integer cents. Every constant in §5–§8 is implemented in `tools/matchlib.py`; every worked example in §9–§10 is printed by `tools/golden_answers.py` and ends with `OK`. Every number in §11 is printed by `tools/reference_matcher.py` on the frozen fixtures in §12. Items marked **[verify]** are conventions, not facts; each has a D or O entry in `decisions.md`.

## 1. Product scope

A small community foundation with about 400 synthetic constituents in 312 households, 878 gifts over 2024–2026 across four campaigns, and two donor files to import: file A (a gala platform export, 400 rows, split name columns, UTF-8 with BOM, CRLF) and file B (a peer-to-peer run export, 250 rows, one "Name" column in mixed styles, cp1252, Excel serial dates). Each import row carries one gift.

Left out: online giving, recurring gifts, pledge reminders, events, memberships, grants, wealth screening, address verification services, email sending.

## 2. Vocabulary

Follows the Raiser's Edge / Salesforce NPSP vocabulary where they agree **[verify: both products use these terms; exact semantics differ by product and by shop]**.

| Term | Meaning here |
|---|---|
| Constituent | A person on record. Organizations are out of scope (seam: `kind` column, always `PERSON`). |
| Household | A group of constituents at one mailing address, for combined letters and household giving totals. A constituent belongs to at most one household. Household giving = Σ hard credits of its members. |
| Gift | A completed contribution: amount in cents, date, fund, appeal. **Hard credit** goes to the constituent who gave it. |
| Soft credit | Recognition of a constituent who influenced a gift but did not give it (a spouse, a donor-advised-fund advisor, a peer-to-peer fundraiser). Never counted in raised totals; counted in a constituent's recognition total. |
| Pledge | A promise to give, with a schedule. Pledge payments are gifts with `pledgeId`; pledge balance is derived. |
| Fund | *Where the money may be used*: the designation or restriction (General, Building, Scholarship). NPSP calls this a General Accounting Unit. |
| Campaign | The umbrella effort with a goal and a date range ("2026 Annual Fund"). |
| Appeal | The specific ask that produced the gift ("Spring Mailer 2026", "Gala 2026"). An appeal belongs to one campaign; a gift references one appeal. |
| Acknowledgement letter | The thank-you letter for a gift. **Sample only.** |
| Import batch | One uploaded file, its mapping, its preview, and everything its commit wrote. |
| Review item | One candidate pair whose score fell in the review band, awaiting a person. |
| Merge event | One merge of two constituents, with both records' snapshots, reversible. |
| Effective state | The current view of the org after all superseding rows are applied (D-11). |

**US acknowledgment rules, cited not implemented.** The IRS says a donor claiming a deduction of $250 or more must obtain a contemporaneous written acknowledgment that states whether the charity provided any goods or services in return and, if so, a good-faith estimate of their value; separately, a charity must give a written disclosure for a quid pro quo contribution over $75. Source: IRS, "Charitable Contributions – Written Acknowledgments" (irs.gov/node/551, read 2026-10-14) and Publication 1771. Housefile prints the sample sentence "No goods or services were provided in exchange for this contribution" on every letter and makes no claim that its letters satisfy these rules.

## 3. Synthetic population and defect catalog

Generated by `tools/build_fixtures.py --seed 20261014` (§12). Persons are invented: names from fixed lists (including diacritics: Renée, José, Zoë, Anaïs, Björn), US addresses in seven cities (Tulsa and Edmond weighted; Newark NJ, Worcester MA and Burlington VT included for their leading-zero ZIPs; Denver and Austin), phones only in 555-0100..555-0199 (the block reserved for fictional use) across seven area codes, emails only on RFC 2606 documentation domains (`example.com/.org/.net`, `mail.example.com`). No `gmail.com` address is generated: an invented name at a real provider could be someone's real mailbox, so Gmail dot-folding is exercised by GN-01 only.

**Population:** 300 base households; 30% have a second member (same surname 70% of the time); of those, 35% share one family email and 50% share a phone. 15% of persons carry a prior surname; 20% a prior address; 6% a PO box. 70% of persons are already in the CRM; 10% of CRM records store a nickname as the first name; 45% of CRM records have a DOB.

**Not generated (say so in any write-up):** OCR noise, hyphenated double surnames, cultural name-order conventions (family name first), international addresses, organizations as donors, donor-advised-fund intermediaries, the same person with two genuinely different emails in one file, deceased flags, "Mr. & Mrs." joint rows, duplicate gifts with slightly different amounts (fees deducted). Each is a candidate for the hand-written file in P1.

## 4. Import pipeline

| # | Stage | Decides | Output |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Parse | Encoding (BOM → UTF-8; decode error → cp1252), delimiter, header row | Raw rows + content hash on the batch |
| 2 | Map | Which column is which field; synonyms below; unmapped → `extras` | Saved column map |
| 3 | Normalize | N1–N9 per field | Normalized record + rules fired |
| 4 | Validate | `OK` / `WARN:<code>` / `REJECT:<code>` | Rejected rows stop here |
| 5 | Block | Candidate set from B1–B5 against CRM + accepted rows of this batch | Pairs to score |
| 6 | Score | §7 | Score, reasons, flags, decision per pair |
| 7 | Decide | Best candidate's decision; survivorship plan for a merge | Row outcome |
| 8 | Preview | Every proposed write, counted | `beforeHash` stored |
| 9 | Commit | Exactly the preview, one transaction | Rows tagged with the batch |
| 10 | Review | Queue of review-band pairs | Human decisions, audited |
| 11 | Undo | Compensating rows; `stateHash == beforeHash` proven | Batch marked undone |

**Header synonyms the mapper must know** (case-insensitive, punctuation ignored): first ← `first name`, `donor first name`, `fname`, `given`; last ← `last name`, `surname`, `lname`, `family`; full ← `name`, `donor name`, `full name`, `donor`; email ← `email`, `e-mail`, `email address`; phone ← `phone`, `phone #`, `telephone`, `mobile`, `cell`; line1 ← `address`, `address 1`, `street`, `street address`, `addr1`; line2 ← `address 2`, `apt`, `apt/unit`, `unit`, `suite`; city; state ← `state`, `st`, `province`; zip ← `zip`, `zip code`, `postal`, `postal code`, `zipcode`; dob ← `dob`, `birth date`, `birthdate`, `date of birth`; gift_date ← `gift date`, `date`, `gift dt`, `transaction date`; amount ← `amount`, `gift amount`, `amt`, `total`, `donation`; fund ← `fund`, `designation`, `program`; external_ref ← `transaction id`, `txn`, `reference`, `confirmation`. A map that leaves `amount`, `gift_date`, or both name fields unmapped is refused.

**Validation codes (closed set).** REJECT: `NAME_MISSING`, `AMOUNT_MISSING`, `AMOUNT_INVALID`, `GIFT_DATE_MISSING`, `GIFT_DATE_INVALID`, `DATE_FUTURE` (after the batch's as-of date). WARN: `EMAIL_INVALID` (email dropped, row continues), `PHONE_INVALID`, `ZIP_PADDED`, `ZIP_INVALID`, `STATE_INVALID`, `ADDRESS_UNPARSED`, `AMOUNT_NEGATIVE` (kept; shown as a refund candidate, not auto-applied), `AMOUNT_ZERO`, `DOB_DATE_INVALID`, `DOB_DATE_EXCEL_SERIAL`, `DATE_EXCEL_SERIAL`, `FUND_UNKNOWN` (fund text not in `import/fund-synonyms.json`; the batch's default fund is used and the raw text kept in `extras`). A rejected row writes nothing — no constituent, no gift — and is not matched or indexed (D-32); the person fixes the file and re-imports.

## 5. Normalization rules

| ID | Field | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| N1 | Names | NFKD, strip diacritics, lowercase, collapse spaces, drop punctuation; an apostrophe is dropped, a hyphen becomes a space (`Smith-Jones` → `smith jones`; D-40a). Parse `Last, First M. Suffix` and `First M. Last Suffix`. Suffix ∈ {JR, SR, II, III, IV}, read from its own column, from the end of the last name, or from any token of a full name. A second token in the first-name column is a middle name. **Canonical first names:** the nickname table maps a raw first name to the *set* of canonical names it can stand for (Bob → {Robert}; Ted → {Edward, Theodore}; Sam → {Samuel, Samantha}); an unknown name maps to itself. Two first names agree when their sets intersect. |
| N2 | Email | Lowercase, strip spaces; exactly one `@`; `+tag` removed on every domain; on `gmail.com`/`googlemail.com` dots in the local part removed and domain set to `gmail.com` (O-4). Invalid → `EMAIL_INVALID`, value dropped. |
| N3 | Phone | Digits only; 11 digits starting with 1 → drop the 1; anything but 10 digits → `PHONE_INVALID`. |
| N4 | Address | Uppercase the number, street tokens and unit (GN-19: `N CHURCH LN`, `33C`; D-40b); USPS-style abbreviations (Street→ST, Avenue→AVE, Road→RD, Drive→DR, Lane→LN, Court→CT, Boulevard→BLVD, Place→PL, Circle→CIR, Terrace→TER, Parkway→PKWY, Trail→TRL, Highway→HWY; North→N …); leading token `\d+[a-z]?` is the number; a trailing `apt|apartment|unit|ste|suite|#|no|rm|room|fl|floor <x>` on line 1, or line 2 as a whole, is the unit with the designator word and hyphens removed, uppercased; `P.O. Box / PO Box / Post Office Box / POB <n>` → `pobox = n` (leading zeros dropped); `line1_key = "<number> <STREET>"` or `"PO BOX <n>"`. City uppercased. |
| N5 | ZIP | Digits of the part before any hyphen; 9 digits → first 5; 3–4 digits → left-pad to 5 (`ZIP_PADDED`); else `ZIP_INVALID`. |
| N6 | Dates | `YYYY-MM-DD`; `M/D/YYYY`; `M/D/YY` (00–69 → 20xx, 70–99 → 19xx; for a **birth date** a 20xx reading after `asOf` is 19xx instead, D-43); 5-digit integer 20000–60000 → Excel serial from 1899-12-30 (`DATE_EXCEL_SERIAL`); `Mon D, YYYY` and `D Mon YYYY`. US month-first; `13/02/2026` is `DATE_INVALID`, not guessed (`DATE_INVALID` is the normalizer's code; a gift date that fails rejects the row as `GIFT_DATE_INVALID`, §4; D-40c). |
| N7 | Amounts | Strip `$`, `USD`, commas, spaces; `(x)` or leading `-` is negative (`AMOUNT_NEGATIVE`); at most two decimals → integer cents; zero → `AMOUNT_ZERO`; anything else `AMOUNT_INVALID`. Never a float anywhere. |
| N8 | State | Two-letter code uppercased, or full name → code; else `STATE_INVALID`. |
| N9 | Encoding | Strip BOM; decode UTF-8, fall back to cp1252; record which. |

## 6. Similarity and blocking

- **Jaro-Winkler** (standard: matching window `max(len)/2 − 1`, transpositions halved, prefix scale 0.1, max prefix 4), rounded to 4 places. `jon`/`john` = 0.9333.
- **American Soundex** (first letter kept; B F P V→1, C G J K Q S X Z→2, D T→3, L→4, M N→5, R→6; H and W transparent; vowels reset; 4 characters). `Flores` = `Florse` = F462 — a transposition typo usually survives blocking.

A pair is scored only when the two records share at least one blocking key:

| Key | Built from | Catches |
|---|---|---|
| B1 | normalized email | exact identifier |
| B2 | phone digits | exact identifier, shared landlines |
| B3 | Soundex(last) + ZIP5 | typos in the surname at the same address |
| B4 | `line1_key` + ZIP5 | same building: households, Jr/Sr, surname changes, unit-format noise |
| B5 | Soundex(each canonical first) + Soundex(last) | moved donors, nicknames, email/phone changes |

No key: transposed name + surname change + no phone + no address (B-0154 in §11). That row is reported as "not blocked", not hidden.

## 7. Scoring model (`matching/model.v1.json`)

Additive points; a pair's score is the sum of the features that fire. Features fire only when both sides have the field.

| Feature | Points | Fires when |
|---|---|---|
| EMAIL_EXACT | +45 | normalized emails equal |
| PHONE_EXACT | +25 | phone digits equal |
| FIRST_EXACT | +15 | folded first names equal |
| FIRST_NICKNAME | +15 | not equal, canonical sets intersect |
| FIRST_CLOSE | +10 | JW ≥ 0.90 |
| FIRST_INITIAL | +5 | one side is a single letter equal to the other's first letter |
| FIRST_DIFF | −25 | JW < 0.80, or initials differ |
| SURNAME_EXACT | +15 | equal |
| SURNAME_CLOSE | +10 | JW ≥ 0.90 |
| SURNAME_DIFF | −10 | JW < 0.80 (mild: surnames change) |
| NAME_TRANSPOSED | +25 | A.first ≈ B.last and B.first ≈ A.last (canonical); replaces the first/surname features |
| SUFFIX_EXACT | +5 | both present and equal |
| SUFFIX_CONFLICT | −40 | both present and different → **hard block** |
| SUFFIX_ONE_SIDED | −10 | exactly one side has a suffix |
| MIDDLE_CONFLICT | −15 | both have a middle and the initials differ |
| ADDRESS_EXACT | +20 | `line1_key`, unit and ZIP5 equal |
| ADDRESS_SAME_BUILDING | +15 | `line1_key` and ZIP5 equal, unit differs or missing |
| ZIP_EXACT | +3 | ZIP5 equal, nothing above fired |
| DOB_EXACT | +20 | equal |
| DOB_DIFF | −30 | both present, different |

**Decision:**
1. `SUFFIX_CONFLICT` fired → `NEW`, flag `HARD_BLOCK_SUFFIX` (Jr and Sr are never merged by the machine).
2. score ≥ **70** but `FIRST_DIFF` fired → `REVIEW`, flag `FIRST_NAME_CONFLICT_CAP`; score ≥ 70 but `DOB_DIFF` fired → `REVIEW`, flag `DOB_CONFLICT_CAP`. The machine never merges two clearly different first names or two different birth dates, however many identifiers agree: a couple sharing email, phone, surname and address scores 80 (GA-19) and is two people.
3. score ≥ **70** → `MERGE`.
4. score ≥ **45** → `REVIEW`.
5. else `NEW`, except two **floors** that lift a pair to `REVIEW`: `IDENTIFIER_FLOOR` when EMAIL_EXACT or PHONE_EXACT fired (a shared identifier is never silently ignored), and `NAME_CHANGE_CANDIDATE` when ADDRESS_EXACT and FIRST_EXACT/FIRST_NICKNAME fired (same person at the same address under a new surname, or a namesake relative).
6. Flag `HOUSEHOLD_CANDIDATE` on a `NEW` with ADDRESS_EXACT and either FIRST_DIFF or SUFFIX_CONFLICT; the review/constituent UI offers "link as household" for these.

What the numbers mean: no single field reaches 70; email plus an agreeing name does (45 + 15 + 15 = 75); phone plus name needs the address too (25 + 30 + 20 = 75); name plus address alone is 50 and goes to a person (GA-02) because a father and son without suffixes look exactly like that. The caps in step 2 were added after the first fixture run auto-merged four spouse pairs per file that shared email, phone, surname and address (decisions.md §Review log). The thresholds were set by hand and then checked against the fixtures (§11); they are O-2 until Shane's labels (Phase 1 task 1.9) are in.

**Within a batch:** rows are resolved in file order against the CRM and the rows already accepted. On a `MERGE` the target absorbs the identifiers it lacked (email, phone, DOB, address when empty — S1) and the row's blocking keys point at the target, so a later duplicate of the same person finds it. A `REVIEW` row is indexed as itself. A `REJECT` row is not indexed.

## 8. Survivorship, merge, undo

**Survivorship (field-level, on a MERGE of an import row into an existing constituent, or of two constituents):**

| ID | Rule |
|---|---|
| S1 | Non-empty beats empty. |
| S2 | Email, phone, address: the value with the newer `asOf` wins (an import row's `asOf` is the batch's file date); the loser is kept as an alternate with its source and `asOf`. (O-5) |
| S3 | Names: the existing record's name wins; the import's spelling is kept as an alias. A suffix is never added or removed by survivorship. DOB: existing wins; a conflict was already a review reason. |
| S4 | Gifts are never deduplicated by survivorship. An import gift that equals an existing gift of the same constituent on date, amount and `externalRef` (or date and amount when the file has no reference) is skipped as `DUP_GIFT` and listed in the preview; in-file byte-identical duplicates skip their gift as `DUP_GIFT_IN_FILE`. Anything less exact becomes a gift. |
| S5 | Household: a merge keeps the existing household; `SAME_HOUSEHOLD` links the new constituent to the target's household (creating one if needed) without merging. |
| S6 | Existing-to-existing merges (one row matches two CRM records at MERGE level) are never automatic; both pairs go to review with flag `EXISTING_PAIR`. |

**Merge event.** Stores the effective state of both records (all fields, aliases, alternates, household, gift ids, soft-credit ids) before the merge, the survivorship choices, who and why. `unmerge` re-creates the loser from its snapshot as a new version, reverts the winner's fields to its snapshot, moves gifts back. Proof: each record's effective-state hash equals its snapshot hash. Refused with `UNMERGE_BLOCKED` if a later merge event touched either record.

**Effective-state hash (D-11).** Canonical JSON (sorted keys, integers, ISO dates) of every active constituent's effective fields and household, every active gift, every soft credit and household link, sorted by id; SHA-256. Row ids, `createdAt`, batch ids and audit rows are excluded so a commit followed by its undo hashes equal.

**Undo a batch.** For every write tagged with the batch: created constituents → retired version (not deleted; O-7); field changes → a new version equal to the pre-batch version; gifts → superseding `VOIDED` rows with reason `BATCH_UNDONE`; merges → unmerge; household links → removed. Then assert `stateHash() == batch.beforeHash`; on mismatch the transaction rolls back and the batch is marked `UNDO_FAILED` with the two hashes. Refused with `UNDO_BLOCKED_BY_LATER_BATCH` when any touched constituent has a later batch's write.
